# e-CNY Pilots

The digital renminbi advances by "pilots first, rollout later": the People's Bank of China selects representative regions and scenarios for controlled testing, verifying theoretical reliability, system stability, functional usability, process convenience, scenario fit and risk controllability, and only then expands the scope. This page gathers the officially published pilot timeline, scale figures and cross-border trials. For the mechanism design, see [the digital renminbi](/dcny/Dcny).

## The Four Essentials

- **Which layer**: the application layer — the pilots are the verification surface of the issuance system in real scenarios and do not change the centralized architecture of the [settlement layer](/layers/Settlement)
- **What it solves**: there is no precedent for a retail central bank digital currency, so designs such as two-tier operation, controllable anonymity and dual offline must be verified in real user and merchant environments
- **How well it solves it**: the pilots expanded from 4 cities plus the Winter Olympics scenario to 15 provinces (municipalities), and transaction volume grew from RMB 34.5 billion in mid-2021 to RMB 100.04 billion by August 2022; officials explicitly "set no preset timetable for release" — the rollout pace is decided by pilot evaluation
- **Source**: PBoC [e-CNY R&D pilot topic page](http://www.pbc.gov.cn/redianzhuanti/118742/4657542/4678070/index.html) and the [*Progress of R&D of E-CNY in China* white paper](http://www.pbc.gov.cn/goutongjiaoliu/113456/113469/4293590/2021071614200022055.pdf)

## Pilot Timeline

| Date | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 2014 | The PBoC set up a legal digital currency research group, starting research on the issuance framework, key technologies and circulation environment |
| 2016 | The digital currency research institute was established and the first-generation prototype system completed; the top-level design proposed two-tier operation, M0 positioning, loose coupling with bank accounts and controllable anonymity |
| End of 2017 | With the approval of the State Council, the PBoC organized commercial banks and relevant institutions to jointly carry out R&D trials of the digital renminbi (DC/EP) |
| 2018 | The PBoC formulated the R&D framework for the digital renminbi as the foundational guidance for the R&D work |
| End of 2019 | Pilot testing started in Shenzhen, Suzhou, Xiong'an New Area and Chengdu |
| November 2020 | Six more pilot regions added: Shanghai, Hainan, Changsha, Xi'an, Qingdao and Dalian |
| 16 July 2021 | The PBoC e-CNY R&D working group published the *Progress of R&D of E-CNY in China* white paper |
| 4 January 2022 | The digital renminbi (pilot edition) app went on sale in the Apple App Store and Android app stores |
| February 2022 | Beijing Winter Olympics scenario pilot — overseas visitors in China could use the digital renminbi |

## Scale Figures (Official)

- **As of 30 June 2021** (white paper): over 1.32 million pilot scenarios, covering utilities payment, catering, transport, shopping, government services and more; more than 20.87 million personal wallets and more than 3.51 million corporate wallets opened; more than 70.75 million transactions cumulatively, worth about RMB 34.5 billion.
- **As of 31 August 2022** (pilot topic page): across the 15 provinces (municipalities) with pilots, cumulative transactions reached 360 million, worth RMB 100.04 billion; 10 designated operating institutions were confirmed through comprehensive evaluation.
- Pilot regions were chosen with overall consideration of the national development strategies, regional coordination strategies and the industrial and economic characteristics of each region, covering the Yangtze River Delta, the Pearl River Delta, Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei, the central, western, northeastern and northwestern regions.

## Scenarios and Ecosystem

- **Red packet campaigns**: during the pilots, local governments issued digital renminbi red packets in many regions (for example, 200,000 red packet reservations in Chengdu), enabling large-scale concentrated testing in batches and verifying system stability and product usability.
- **Winter Olympics scenario**: pilot deployments inside the Beijing Winter Olympics organising committee park included unmanned vending vehicles, self-service vending machines and unmanned supermarkets; wearable devices such as payment gloves, payment badges and Winter Olympics payment apparel were released; overseas visitors could open a digital renminbi wallet without opening a mainland Chinese bank account.
- **Smart contract applications**: smart contract technology gives the digital renminbi programmability, exploring conditional payment in scenarios such as prepaid fund management and designated-use payment.
- **Acceptance-side retrofitting**: commercial banks and licensed non-bank payment institutions may participate in the digital renminbi payment service system, provided they comprehensively and continuously comply with regulatory requirements (anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist financing) and risk supervision, and with the recognition and support of the central bank.

## Cross-Border Trials

- **Multilateral CBDC bridge (mCBDC Bridge)**: the PBoC's digital currency research institute participates together with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, the Bank of Thailand, the central bank of the UAE and the BIS Innovation Hub Hong Kong Centre, exploring the applicability of central bank digital currencies in cross-border payments.
- **Cross-border principles**: follow the three requirements of "no harm", "compliance" and "interoperability"; establish legal digital currency exchange arrangements and regulatory cooperation mechanisms with the relevant monetary authorities and central banks; insist on two-tier operation, risk-based management and modular design.
- **Positioning**: the digital renminbi is currently used mainly to meet domestic retail payment needs; cross-border use is technically possible but not the current focus.

## Failure Modes

- **Unpredictable rollout pace**: officials explicitly "set no preset timetable for release" — pilot evaluation conclusions decide the rollout pace, and policy uncertainty affects merchants' and users' willingness to invest.
- **The cost of user habit migration**: Alipay and WeChat Pay already cover the great majority of retail scenarios, so the digital renminbi needs differentiated value (legal tender status, offline payment, programmability) to migrate users.
- **Cross-border coordination cost**: the mCBDC bridge involves monetary authorities and regulatory cooperation across several countries; exchange arrangements and compliance recognition are a long-term project.
- **Major security incidents**: no public record found.
