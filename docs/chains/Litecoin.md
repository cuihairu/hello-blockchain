# Litecoin

Litecoin (LTC) is a digital currency built on an open-source cryptographic protocol, intended as an improved version of Bitcoin. The following is a detailed introduction to the Litecoin project:

## Whitepaper and Specifications

No formal whitepaper — the 2011-10-13 forum post by Charlie Lee plus the source code ([github.com/litecoin-project/litecoin](https://github.com/litecoin-project/litecoin)) is the specification. Mainnet launched on 2011-10-13.

## What Problem It Solves

Bitcoin confirms slowly (10 minutes) and mining had become GPU/ASIC-dominated — Litecoin uses a faster block interval and a different PoW algorithm, a "complement rather than a replacement."

## Technical Achievements and Contributions

- **Scrypt PoW**: a memory-hard algorithm to slow down ASICs (which eventually beat it anyway) — see [PoW](/consensus/POW).
- **2.5-minute blocks + 84M cap**: parameter-level differentiation as an experiment.
- **First mainnet to activate SegWit (2017-05)**: a live-network rehearsal for Bitcoin's segregated witness upgrade.
- **MWEB (2022-05)**: extension-block private transfers (contrast the in-protocol privacy route of [mixing services](/Privacy/MixingServices)).

## Limitations and Public Incidents

- Technical innovation has mostly been follow-the-leader, and adoption has declined steadily.
- Public incidents: none found in the public record (the bogus Walmart partnership news of 2021-09 was market manipulation, not a chain failure).

## Supplementary Notes
### An Overview of Litecoin

1. **Origins and development**:
   - Litecoin was created in October 2011 by former Google engineer Charlie Lee. It is a fork of Bitcoin and uses a blockchain technology and a decentralized Proof of Work (PoW) consensus mechanism similar to Bitcoin's.

2. **Blockchain technology**:
   - Litecoin uses blockchain technology closely related to Bitcoin's, with a few key differences. For example, Litecoin produces blocks four times as fast as Bitcoin — one block every 2.5 minutes, against Bitcoin's 10 minutes. This makes Litecoin transaction confirmations faster and better suited to everyday payments.

3. **Consensus algorithm**:
   - Litecoin originally used Scrypt as its proof-of-work algorithm, where Bitcoin uses SHA-256. Scrypt strikes a balance between computing demands and security, letting ordinary users mine with CPUs or GPUs instead of being restricted to dedicated ASIC miners.

4. **Features and advantages**:
   - **Fast transaction confirmation**: Litecoin's short block time means faster confirmations, making it suitable as a digital currency for everyday payments.
   - **Low fees**: thanks to fast confirmations and lighter network congestion, Litecoin transaction fees are typically low.
   - **Community support**: Litecoin has an active developer community and user base supporting its technical development and ecosystem.

5. **Use cases and applications**:
   - As a popular cryptocurrency, Litecoin is widely used for trading and payments, especially where fast confirmation and low cost matter. It also serves as a quote pair on some exchanges, traded against other crypto assets.

6. **Development and future plans**:
   - The Litecoin team continues to work on improving network security, scalability, and user experience. New technical features or improvements may be introduced in future to meet market demand and technological progress.

### Summary

As one of Bitcoin's earliest forks, Litecoin became an important member of the cryptocurrency market by offering fast transaction confirmation and low-cost payments. Its development has been backed by broad community support and steady technical innovation, contributing to the diversity and evolution of the digital currency ecosystem.
