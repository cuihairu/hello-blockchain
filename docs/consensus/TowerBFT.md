# TowerBFT

Tower BFT is Solana's BFT voting consensus built on top of PoH (Proof of History), evolved from PBFT/Tendermint; its core innovation is to turn voting itself into a "collateralized commitment with a timeout", using the global time ordering provided by PoH to compress message complexity sharply.

## Four Key Points

- **Which layer**: [Settlement layer](/layers/Settlement) — BFT voting on top of the global clock of [PoH](/consensus/PoH), where voting is a collateralized commitment with a timeout
- **What it solves**: Use the deterministic time ordering provided by PoH to turn voting into a commitment where "changing your mind after staking costs something" — each repeated vote locks in more collateral, compressing message complexity
- **How well it works**: Message complexity is markedly lower than classic BFT, supporting Solana's high throughput and fast confirmations; the cost is a strong dependence on the correctness of PoH ordering, vote timeout parameters that must match network latency, and complex fork-choice and optimistic-confirmation rules. Public incidents: Solana has had several network-wide outages (about 17 hours in 2021-09, about 5 hours in 2024-02, and so on), all accompanied by stalls in voting and confirmation, and TowerBFT's liveness has repeatedly been tested under extreme load and upgrade defects
- **Sources**: "Tower BFT" (an appendix of the Solana whitepaper), Laine and Larsson 2018; Solana developer documentation

## Interplay with PoH

PoH is continuously generated by the leader as a verifiable delay hash sequence, providing **a-priori ordering** for all network events. Tower BFT validators no longer communicate repeatedly about "the order" and only vote on "the hash value at a PoH height" — messages are small and rounds are few.

## Unique Vote Lock Design

- A validator's $k$-th consecutive vote on a fork produces a **lockout of $2^{k-1}$ slots**: it must wait for the lockout to expire before reverting to an earlier fork.
- Voting consecutively along one chain doubles the lockout exponentially (at most 31 levels, a maximum lockout of about 2 to the 30th power slots), which makes "flip-flopping" votes economically infeasible — this replaces traditional BFT locking rules and slashing.
- Votes older than 31 levels are discarded, and validators keep rolling their votes.

## Safety and Liveness

- **Confirmed**: blocks with 2/3 or more of the voting weight are reachable in about 1~2 seconds; **finalized**: irreversible once a vote-lock depth of 31 subsequent slots is reached.
- Tolerates less than 1/3 malicious weight; a failed leader is taken over by a backup leader within a few slots (no explicit view change subprotocol, relying on slot-schedule rotation).

## Trade-offs against Classic BFT

It gives up PBFT-style explicit three phases and instant finality in exchange for: message volume approximately decoupled from validator count, and a 400 ms block interval; the cost is that finality requires observing vote depth, and historically several outages have resulted from fork-choice / client defects (recovery depending on coordinated restarts and checkpoints).
